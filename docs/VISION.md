# What we are trying to do with Nadilo

## The person we are making it for

Someone with a song in their head. They have no instrument, they have never had a music lesson, and words like
"chord" or "tempo" make them feel like music is a club they are not in.

We think they are more musical than they realise. Nadilo is here to show them.

## The problem

There are many good music tools, but none of them quite helps this person.

- **Music apps for making songs** (GarageBand, Ableton, FL Studio) are powerful, but they hand you the whole studio
  at once. They assume you already know what a bass line or a chord is for. The learning happens somewhere else,
  usually on YouTube.
- **Music lessons and toys** (like Ableton's Learning Music or Chrome Music Lab) are friendly and free, but each
  lesson is its own little sandbox. You come out understanding something, but without a song of your own.
- **AI song generators** turn a sentence into a finished song in seconds. But you learn nothing about how it was
  made, so you can't point at a part and change it on purpose.
- **Instrument trainers** (like Melodics or Yousician) teach your hands to play other people's music, not how to
  make your own.

## What Nadilo does instead

**You make one song, all the way through.** Every level adds a part to the same track: first the drums, then the
bass, then the chords, then the melody, and finally you shape it all into a song with an intro, a chorus and an
ending. Nothing you make is a throwaway exercise.

**You hear a song, rebuild it, then make it yours.** School starts by playing you a short song, whole. Each level
rebuilds one part of it: first by tracing, then by carrying a pattern on, then by ear. Once a part is rebuilt, you
pull it away from the song with your own colours, sounds, takes and arrangement. Copying is how musicians have always
learned (it is how pop musicians learn, and how producers practise); the copy is only where your song starts. Stars
say how close you came, and you never need all three to move on. (Why, and the research behind it:
[GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md).)

**You do it first, then you learn its name.** A level never starts with a word you don't know. You light four
pads and hear a heartbeat; only then does Nadilo tell you that thump is called a kick drum. The name is the reward,
not the homework.

**One new idea at a time, in the order a good teacher would use.** Pulse, then the bass, then colour, then a
tune, then the story. Each world builds on the last.

**The app grows with you.** Level one is four big pads. By the end you are using a drum machine, a bass line, a
chord picker, a melody ladder and an arranger: a small music studio that you put together yourself, piece by piece.

## What we believe

- **Fun is the feeling of learning a pattern.** Once you have mastered one, the next level hands you a slightly
  harder one. This comes from Raph Koster's *A Theory of Fun for Game Design*.
- **Show, don't tell, and never make them guess.** A goal names what to touch ("Tap the four outlined Boom pads."),
  and the song is always one press away. A riddle of a goal is as bad as a paragraph.
- **Ears before words.** The screen stays quiet: a title, one short goal, and the instrument. The longer explanations
  live in a guide (`docs/school.md`) for anyone who wants them.
- **Calm, not busy.** Everything has its place and stays there. Nothing jumps around, so your eyes can stay on the
  music.
- **A sparring partner, not an autopilot.** When you finish a level, Nadilo offers three other takes on your part.
  You listen, compare, and keep one or ignore them all. It never changes your song behind your back. (This idea
  comes from *Sentient Sketchbook*, a design tool that suggests alternatives to a designer.)
- **Good reasons to come back, not guilt.** You come back because you want to hear your song grow, or to get the
  third star. No streaks, no lives, no timers, no points to lose: stars only ever go up.
- **It has to feel like an instrument.** A pad plays the moment you press it, not when you let go.
- **It has to sound good on a laptop.** Most people will hear Nadilo through their laptop's speakers, so every sound
  was chosen and measured to work there.
- **Your work is yours.** No account, no sign-up. Everything is saved on your own computer.

## What is in it today

- **Nadilo School:** hear a song, rebuild it, then make it yours: five worlds and fifteen levels, one bar at a time,
  ending on your own song, and a small twist to it each day.
  - **Pulse:** the beat, from one kick drum to a full drum kit with ghost notes, swing and your own sounds.
  - **The Floor:** the bass line, locked to the kick and following the mood.
  - **Color:** chords, shown as colours, and how a song "comes home".
  - **Voice:** a melody, a motif that repeats, and an ending that lands.
  - **Story:** turning parts on and off over a whole song: build up, break down, let go.
- **Ear training:** thirteen short games where you hear a hidden beat and rebuild it by ear.

## Where it could go next

These are open questions, not promises:

- **Hum it in.** Sing an idea into the microphone and watch it appear on the melody ladder. This is the most direct
  route from a song in your head to a song you can hear.
- **Take your song with you.** Save your finished song as an audio file to share.
- **A bigger studio.** After the last world, open your same song in a fuller studio view, with more bars, more sounds
  and more control.
- **Classrooms.** Find out whether Nadilo is first for people learning at home or for music teachers, because
  that changes what it needs.
- **Watch real beginners use it.** Give it to five people with no instructions, see where they get stuck, and ask
  whether they can explain each level in their own words afterwards.
